Hi, I'm Natalie! I am a senior mechanical engineering student at UT Austin interested in building physical products that work well not only technically, but in the context of the people who actually use them.

01 — Background

Background

I was drawn to mechanical engineering because I liked understanding how things worked, but my experiences at UT have shaped that curiosity into a stronger interest in product development and the people behind it.

In the BioMATTER Lab, working with tissue, bone, and bioprinted materials showed me how meaningful technical work can be when there is a clear human outcome. Design and manufacturing coursework then pulled me toward the physical side of engineering, taking ideas from CAD to machined, tested, and iterated parts. I learned how manufacturing constraints shape a realistic design.

My work in the Marras Research Group showed me that I'm just as interested in software when it makes a technical process simpler and more accessible. At Rimkus Engineering, I approached problems from the opposite direction, working backward from real failures and combining technical observations and context to understand the larger system.

Outside of engineering, my tutoring business, guiding with the Outdoor Center, and student leadership have reinforced how much I value collaboration and communication. Together, these experiences have drawn me toward work where technical problem solving, creative design, and teamwork come together to create something genuinely useful for people.

02 — Projects

Research & Projects

DNA Origami to Printable STL Application

Marras Research Group, UT Austin · May 2026 – Present

Marras Research Group works with DNA origami — nanoscale structures built from strands of DNA — but physically prototyping those designs at a larger scale required specialized software to convert complex molecular geometry into printable models. I coded a Python pipeline that processes tens of thousands of atomic coordinates and converts raw PDB models into simplified, 3D-printable geometry. I then iterated through printing challenges involving flatness, adhesion, geometry, and scale across different structures. What I like most about the project is that the result isn't just a successful print, but an automated tool that makes physical prototyping simpler and more accessible for the researchers using it. I'm currently building the front end of the application so the rest of the lab can use the workflow without needing to manipulate the code.

Python · Claude Code · Geometry Processing · Additive Manufacturing · Rapid Prototyping

RC Car Tractor-Pull Redesign

Machine Elements · Spring 2026

Our team redesigned an RC car to improve tractor-pull performance by increasing traction, torque, and drivetrain strength. I used machine-element calculations, FEA, CAD, and prototyping to guide changes to the gear ratio, rear shafts, wheel interfaces, and rims, then tested how those changes affected performance. The final design increased average tractor-pull capacity by 29%, from 600 g to 776.7 g.

SolidWorks · FEA · Machine Design · Prototyping · Gearing · Design for Manufacturing

Machined Mechanical Vise

Machine Tool Operations · Spring 2026

I manufactured a complete mechanical vise from aluminum and brass stock using manual mills, lathes, CNC machining, a drop saw, and press fitting. Machining every surface, hole, thread, fit, and moving component into one functioning assembly gave me a much stronger understanding of tolerancing and design for manufacturing than CAD alone. Now when I design something, I naturally think about tool selection, material properties, tolerances, datum selection, and how the part will actually be manufactured.

Manual Milling · Lathe · CNC Machining · Drop Saw · Tolerancing

Mechanical Characterization of Biomaterials

BioMATTER Lab, UT Austin · October 2023 – September 2024

My first long-term engineering research experience put me at the intersection of mechanics and biology, studying age-related degeneration in musculoskeletal tissue and bone. We explored bioprinted materials and structures for future minimally invasive treatments. I helped develop research plans, prepared and imaged samples, performed stress–strain testing, printed GelMA scaffolds, and created experimental schematics for publication. I especially liked that the technical work had a clear human endpoint and that the project required us to decide not only how to test something, but what questions were worth exploring.

Mechanical Testing · Biomaterials · Animal Research · Bioprinting · Stress–Strain Analysis · BioRender
