Case Study

RC Car Redesign for Tractor-Pull

A competition RC car had been optimized for speed, which limited its performance in drawbar-pull events. I led a redesign of the drivetrain and chassis to increase pulling capacity while maintaining acceptable handling on loose, uneven terrain. The revised vehicle pulled 30% more load than the original.

Role
Redesign lead
Timeline
2023–present
Team
Class project team
Tools
SolidWorks, FEA, mill and lathe, FDM printing
Result
30% greater load capacity

Problem

Drawbar-pull performance depends on traction and wheel torque, while the agility and varied-terrain events favor low rotating inertia and a light, responsive vehicle. The original design prioritized top speed. Under load, the drive wheels lost traction early, and chassis deflection was large enough to shift the wheelbase and toe angle.

The redesign needed to increase usable pulling force without adding enough mass or gear reduction to compromise performance on the agility and varied-terrain courses.

Approach

  • Drivetrain. Revised the gear ratio from [start ratio] to [final ratio] to increase wheel torque, sized against motor stall torque and a target pull force.
  • Traction. Shifted weight distribution rearward and revised the drive-wheel tire selection and contact patch to keep the tires below the slip limit at peak pull.
  • Structure. Performed finite element analysis of the chassis and axle-carrier load path under the pull reaction, adding material only where deflection contributed to wheelbase and toe change. Verified critical members with machine-element calculations for bending, bearing, and shear at the pin joints.
  • Prototyping. Modeled components in SolidWorks and produced printed prototypes of the gears, motor stand, and fitted parts, then machined the final load-bearing components on the mill and lathe to specified tolerances.

Results

  • The revised vehicle pulled 30% more load than the original.
  • Chassis deflection at peak pull was reduced to [value], holding wheelbase and toe angle within [tolerance].
  • Machined drivetrain components held [tolerance] on the shaft fits. Printed prototypes identified [issue] before final machining.
  • Agility-course lap time changed by [value], within the acceptable trade-off.

What I would do differently

Earlier instrumentation would have improved the process. Adding current-draw logging and a hitch-mounted load cell at the start would have grounded design decisions in measured data rather than qualitative assessment. I would also design the machined components around available stock and tooling from the outset to avoid a second machining cycle.
